# Guest Seat: the four launch industries + the campaign

Companion to [../guest-seat-spec.md](../guest-seat-spec.md) (the product spec
for the platform). This doc picks the four verticals where the guest seat lands
hardest and lays out the phase 2 marketing campaign aimed at them.

Working name used throughout: **Guest Pass**. Alternatives if it collides:
Partner Seat, Vendor Link. Guest Pass reads best in ad copy ("give your plumber
a Guest Pass") and implies exactly the right power dynamic: guests are invited,
and invitations get revoked.

---

## Part 1: the four industries

Scored on the four things that make the model work: how often outside parties
touch their jobs, how lopsided the power is (our user must hold the work, the
money, or both), how much post-call coordination is worth, and how likely the
guest is to become a full OneBy customer later. All four are verticals we
already have landing pages, themes, and blogs for, so the campaign rides
existing rails.

### 1. Property management (the flagship)

- The loop: tenant calls about a leak -> AI or office takes the call -> work
  order created -> assigned to the plumbing vendor's Guest Pass -> vendor
  accepts, sees the AI summary of what the tenant said ($3.99 layer), goes,
  uploads photos, marks done -> owner report writes itself from the timeline.
- Power dynamic: as lopsided as it gets. The PM company controls deal flow for
  every vendor on its roster. Vendors adopt whatever keeps the work coming.
- Volume: a mid-size PM shop dispatches to plumbers, HVAC, electricians,
  landscapers, cleaners, and handymen weekly. One host can light up 10 to 30
  guest seats, and every single guest is a trade we sell to.
- Conversion kicker: PM vendors ARE our other verticals. This is the vertical
  that seeds all the others.

### 2. Restoration (insurance work)

- The loop: burst pipe at 2am -> call answered -> mitigation starts. Around
  every job orbit an insurance adjuster, a referring plumber, and rebuild subs.
  Adjuster gets a read-only Reviewer pass: timeline, photos, forms, and the
  first-notice-of-loss call summary. The referring plumber gets a Referral
  pass: status on the job they sent (referral partners who can see their
  referrals get paid and updated keep referring). Rebuild subs get Field
  vendor passes.
- Power dynamic: the restoration company owns the loss site and the
  documentation everyone else needs. Adjusters want the paper trail; the
  timeline with timestamps, photos, and the original call recording summary is
  claim gold.
- Post-call value: highest of any vertical. In restoration, what was said on
  the first call is evidence.

### 3. Roofing (adjusters + sub-crews + supplements)

- The loop: storm hits -> calls flood in -> jobs created. Install crews are
  routinely 1099 sub-crews: a Field vendor pass gives the crew lead the
  address, scope, material list (via forms), and schedule, and lets them shoot
  completion photos into the job. The insurance adjuster or supplement partner
  gets a Reviewer pass to the documentation.
- Power dynamic: the roofer holds the homeowner, the claim, and the money.
  Crews follow the work; adjusters follow the documentation.
- Seasonality bonus: after a storm, roofers scale crews fast. Guest Passes at
  $0.99 scale up and down with the season with zero commitment, which is
  exactly how roofers think.

### 4. Law firms (the proof it goes beyond the trades)

- The loop: intake call -> matter opened -> the firm's constellation gets
  scoped passes: process server (task: serve by Friday, address attached),
  private investigator (task list per matter), court reporter (deposition
  appointments), co-counsel or referring attorney (Reviewer pass on shared
  matters). The $3.99 layer shares intake call summaries with co-counsel so
  nobody re-interviews the client.
- Power dynamic: the firm controls the matter and the client. Everyone else is
  engaged per task and works to the firm's deadlines.
- Why include a non-trade vertical in the launch four: it proves Guest Pass is
  a platform capability, not a home-services gimmick, and law firms are our
  highest-rate vertical for the phone product already.

### Bench (fast follows, not launch)

- Dental practices and their dental labs (case ready dates drive appointment
  scheduling; the lab juggling 30 practices is a heavy future host).
- MSP/IT with hardware vendors and ISPs per client site.
- General contractors, once GC exists as a vertical.
- Small developers and custom builders as a host vertical. As hosts their
  wedge fit is weaker (their pain is outbound coordination, not missed
  inbound calls, and construction PM is Procore/Buildertrend territory), so
  they enter as GUESTS first: the recurring commercial client of our trade
  hosts, and the owner seat at scale for PM hosts. A developer who
  accumulates passes from several trades on one build has a project-wide
  view of their own job sitting in OneBy already; that is the warmest host
  upgrade conversation we will ever have. Track multi-host guests and aim
  the "run it from your side" pitch at them.

## Part 2: the campaign

### Positioning

One sentence: **Stop being the switchboard between your customers and your
vendors.**

The pitch under it: every job you dispatch to an outside vendor today runs on
your personal cell: forwarded voicemails, retyped addresses, "did you finish?"
texts. A Guest Pass puts that vendor inside YOUR workspace instead: they see
exactly the jobs you give them, nothing else, and every update lands on your
timeline. You stay in control. They stay in the loop. A dollar a month.

Two sells to the same host, same mechanic:

1. **The vendor sell:** the people you dispatch work to. "They see what you
   share. You see everything. Revoke in one tap."
2. **The regulars sell:** the people you talk to all the time about ongoing
   work. The property owner calling for updates, the facility manager at the
   commercial account, the realtor who refers three jobs a month. Give them a
   pass and they stop calling you for status: "Stop being the update desk."

Boundary to keep in all copy: passes are for recurring relationships, never
one-off customers. Never let it read as a paid customer portal (Jobber gives
a client portal away free; recurring relationships with history are what they
cannot do, because they have no call layer to share).

Three supporting hooks, in priority order:

1. Control: "They see what you share. You see everything. Revoke in one tap."
2. The AI handoff (the $3.99 upsell): "Your vendor reads what the customer
   said on the call before they knock. No retelling, no telephone game."
3. The dollar: "Your whole vendor network, in the loop, for less than a coffee."

### What competitors cannot say (use everywhere)

Phone-only tools have no jobs to share. Field-service tools have no calls to
share. OneBy is the only one where the thing that starts the job, the phone
call, is the thing you can hand to the vendor. That claim is honest today for
summaries-at-launch scope; keep the wording post-call (summaries, transcripts),
not live answering, per the AI sequencing decision.

### Audiences and sequencing

- Primary (paid + organic): PM companies. Biggest seat counts, cleanest story.
- Secondary: restoration and roofing, angled on adjusters and documentation.
- Tertiary (organic only at first): law firms.
- The invisible audience: the guests themselves. Every invite email and every
  locked-module screen in the guest view is an ad we do not pay for. The
  in-product guest experience is a marketing surface and should get the same
  copy discipline as the website ("Powered by OneBy. Run your own business
  here, invoicing is free.").

### Channels

1. Website: a /guest-pass feature page (hero: the switchboard line; a
   two-sided mock showing host view vs. guest view with the guest's screen
   visibly smaller and scoped). Add a Guest Pass section to the four vertical
   pages. Add rows to the compare tables ("Vendor guest seats: $0.99 / not
   offered").
2. The founder flow: add a founder perk, "10 Guest Passes free for 6 months"
   (number pending Moshe). It sweetens the founder offer at near-zero cost and
   pre-loads the viral loop on day one.
3. Email: one dedicated drip email to the existing waitlist, PM-segment first.
   Subject: "Give your plumber a $1 login (that you control)".
4. FB groups (per the group playbook, value-first): the PM groups are the
   target-rich rooms. Question post: "PMs, how do your vendors get work orders
   today, and how often do you retype the same tenant complaint into a text?"
5. Meta ads phase 2 ad set (after the organic read): PM targeting, the
   switchboard hook, destination /guest-pass with UTMs per the ads kit rules.

> plumber at 9pm? OneBy answers the call, writes the work order, and your
> vendor sees it on a $0.99 Guest Pass you control. They update. You watch.

> Your owners don't want a monthly PDF. They want to know the leak in unit 4B

Ad, trades regulars (facility manager / referral partner):

2. Pre-launch: /guest-pass page + vertical page sections + compare rows, all

3. Launch: founder Guest Pass perk live, PM drip email, FB group question

4. Post-launch, once real hosts exist: the case-study engine ("how a 400-door
   PM runs 14 vendors on Guest Passes"), then the Meta ad set.

### KPIs (in order)

1. Guest-to-account conversion rate (the whole point).
2. Seats per host (network density = churn protection).
3. $3.99 attach rate, split by who paid (host vs. guest; guest-paid is the
   more interesting signal).
4. Seat revenue last. If seat revenue is ever the headline number, the
   feature is being run wrong.
